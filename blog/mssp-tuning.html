Working With an MSSP Without Losing the Plot
  • Imran Khalique
  • Detection & Response
  • Mar 2026
  • MDR, SIEM, Reliaquest

The pitch for an MSSP is appealing: 24/7 coverage, deep tooling expertise, an analyst on the other end of the chat at 2 a.m. The reality is that an MSSP is a partnership, and like any partnership it works when both sides are clear about who owns what — and falls apart when the internal team treats it as a black box.

The default failure mode: send a vendor your logs, get back tickets. Acknowledge the tickets. Repeat for two years. By then your team can't remember why a given detection exists, the false positive rate has climbed, and leadership starts asking why you're paying for a service that mostly generates noise. This isn't the MSSP's fault. It's what happens when nobody on the internal side owns the relationship.

For me, three things made the difference between "vendor we pay" and "partnership that gets better over time."

Triage every escalation, not just the high-severity ones. Even a low-severity escalation tells you something about the detection's fidelity. If you only look at the ones the MSSP flagged red, you'll never notice that 30% of the medium-severity escalations are actually noise from one misconfigured rule.

Push back on tuning, but bring evidence. "This rule is too noisy" is not a tuning request. "This rule fired 47 times in the last month, 45 of which were activity from our backup process running between 2 and 3 a.m." is a tuning request. The MSSP can't see what you see; bring the context.

Translate for leadership. The MSSP's monthly report is for you, not your CFO. Take the signal that matters — incidents we'd never have caught alone, time-to-detect, alerts tuned out — and put it in language the executive team can act on.

An MSSP is leverage, not an off-switch for the internal security function. The work shifts from analyst-tier triage to running the partnership. The outcome at Audax: cleaner detections, more credible escalations to leadership, and a working relationship with our MDR provider that actually got better over time instead of decaying into a ticket queue.
